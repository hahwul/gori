# Changelog

## Unreleased

- TUI: an opt-in **Tab numbers** layout pref (`layout.tab_numbers`, Preferences → Layout, off by default) paints `1:`…`9:` before the first nine tabs on the tab bar, the sub-tab strip's convention, so the `1-9 jump` the hint has always advertised has something on screen to point at. The number is the tab's visible position — what the digit keys have always answered to — so a bar scrolled to start at `5:History` still sends `5` there. Off, the bar is byte-identical to before.
- TUI: `y` copies a row on the nine lists that had no copy at all. Sitemap (the cursor row's `host/path`, or every marked row one per line), Discover findings (the URL), Diff rows (endpoint · verdict · what moved), the Issues LIST (`[severity] title (host)`, marks or cursor — the detail's `y` still copies the notes), Colormarker rules (the match filter, pasteable into a bar), Authorize requests (`METHOD host/path`), and the Project tab's env (`KEY=VALUE`), host-override (`ip host`, a hosts-file line) and scope-rule (`kind match-type pattern`) panes. All through the same `read_copy` seam and toast the other copies use (`copied Nb to clipboard`, with the clipboard-off / clipped caveat), and `nothing to copy` on an empty list.
- Sitemap: a double-click on a row's label folds or unfolds a folder, and opens a leaf's flow in the History detail (what `o` does). Expand/collapse answered only on the one-column ▾/▸ marker, where a double-click was a net no-op — the pair's first press toggled and the second toggled back — and the Target tab never forwarded the gesture to the tree at all. The marker column now reads a pair as one toggle, and a double-click off every row is delivered as an ordinary click.
- TUI: the `/` bars take the caret edits the cards already had. `⌃←`/`⌥←` and `⌃→`/`⌥→` move by word, Home/End jump to the line's ends, Delete removes the character under the caret and `⌥⌫` the word before it — on the History, Sitemap, Issues, Probe and Intercept filter bars, the sub-tab strip's filter, and the issue form's title. Each of these kept its own `@query`/caret pair beside `TextField` and answered the modified arrows with a one-character step and the other four keys with nothing; `LineEdit` is the one keymap they share now, with `TextField`'s word rule, so a hand moving between a bar and a card feels no seam. A delete re-runs the bar's filter the way `⌫` does; a motion does not.
- TUI: three idle costs off the render fiber. `Settings.reload_section` stats settings.json before it reads it — the three sections the TUI re-reads on every data_version poll (colour rules, saved views, Match & Replace) were three blocking `File.read`s up to four times a second under capture, and the cache only ever skipped the parse; a file whose mtime and size have not moved is not read. The Notes buffer reloads on that tick only while Notes is the active tab, like every other store-backed view (`on_enter` refreshes a clean buffer on the way back in). And the ui-state identity the 50 ms tick compares is a tuple, not a string interpolated and thrown away every tick.
- TUI: leaving a project stops the Authorize engine too, and an error is a notification, not a one-keypress toast. `Runner#stop_all_jobs` lists the seven job-owning controllers now; Authorize was the one without a `stop_all`, so its engine fiber — which owns its own sockets — kept replaying the batch under every identity after the operator was back at the picker, with the job pinned at `running` and no key that could stop it. A toast raised with `:error` is also written into the notification centre, so the `notify:` chip and the app.notifications card can bring back the one message an operator cannot afford to lose to the next keystroke. A Discover batch the store refused to persist used to be dropped in silence (the rows keep no bytes, so they could never be opened later); the drain says how many exchanges were lost and why. And the activity chip names `authorizing` / `saving results` / `loading results` instead of falling back to `jobs N`.
- Keys: one key, one meaning across the tabs a hand moves between. `d` no longer starts a Discover crawl on the Sitemap — on History, Issues and Probe, one ⇥ away, `d` deletes a row, and the same reflex here put requests on the wire; the crawl is a `space → d` menu row. `⇧X` is the wipe chord and nothing quieter: the Rewriter and Colormarker used it for "enable/disable everywhere", a toggle with no confirm, so the hand trained on the five tab wipes flipped every default rule off where it expected a card; those two are menu rows. `[` / `]` are the Global prev/next-tab chords everywhere now — inside an issue they raised and lowered its severity (hidden, unhinted), on the Rewriter they cycled its sections, and on Discover its runs; severity is a `space → +/-` row, the Rewriter's sections walk on `⇥`/`⇧⇥` (the focus ring, which the bracket keys had stood in for on the reading that the shell owned ⇥ — it does, through this hook), and the RUNS list's own ↑/↓ or two menu rows step Discover's runs. `i` on a read-only pane that sits beside an editor — the Repeater RESPONSE, the Fuzzer RESULTS/DETAIL, the Decoder OUTPUT, the JWT and Cookie DECODED/OUTPUT — used to fall through to the Global `intercept.toggle` and start holding every request on the proxy, with nothing tying that to the keystroke; it now says which pane takes `i` and where intercept is, and stops. `o` opens the row's flow on History, as it does on Sitemap, Probe, Issues, Discover and the Activity feed. The scope lens has one key, the Global `s`: the ⇧S twins on History, Sitemap and Probe were a second key for the same flip in the same tab, and the filter-bar chip now reads `s scope`. `^N` / `^W` open and close a Comparer sub-tab from the body, as on the Decoder, JWT and Cookie bodies. And History's hidden ↑/↓/j/k verbs are gated to History — `Scope::Body` is shared with Help and the Project settings pane, and they were the only Body verbs without the gate.
- TUI: confirm cards answer ↵ with the verb on their button, and the hint strips name keys the keymap actually knows. A card that asks something recoverable — *open in Repeater?*, *run the fuzz?*, *save the results?*, eleven of them — lit `cancel` and answered ↵ with "no" while its button said `open`/`run`/`save`, so the hand that had learnt ↵ = go had to learn `y` for these alone; a non-danger card lights its own verb now, a DANGER card (delete, quit) keeps cancel under ↵, and the hint is spelled from the buttons (`↵ run · y run · n/esc cancel`) rather than a generic `↵ select`. Every picker takes PgUp/PgDn/Home/End, one page being the rows it drew last frame — a 500-flow FlowPicker walked one row at a time. Four keys the OAST providers hint named (`a` add, `e` edit, `x` on/off, `d` delete), Discover's `p` pause and the Fuzzer RESULTS pane's `o` sort / `m` matched / `v` dist were raw `c == 'a'` arms in their controllers: no palette row, no space-menu row, and the hotkey editor offered the letters as free — a binding there would have been silently shadowed. They are chords now (`fuzz.sort`, `fuzz.matched`, `fuzz.dist` are new verbs), the arms are gone, and the hints read them off the keymap. The Sitemap hint no longer lists `space` twice.
- TUI: modals and prompts, made safe under the keys and pastes that reached past them. A confirm card raised over one of the four bottom prompts (`^G` go-to, `^F` find, sub-tab rename, the Repeater tag editor) could not be answered: the prompt claimed `y`, `n` and esc first, so `^D` with *Confirm before quit* on left a QUIT card on screen that nothing could close — the card takes the keys and the click now, and the prompt is still there when it goes. A paste over a modal is delivered key by key, so a pasted line break was ↵: on a one-line form that is COMMIT (pasting `/tmp/a.har⏎` submitted the import and typed what followed into the pane underneath), and on a confirm card a pasted `y` was an answer — `Overlay#takes_pasted?` lets text through and holds back ↵ on a one-line card, takes it as a newline in the four multi-line editors, and takes nothing at all on a confirm; the first held key is named once. A commit closure that opens a child modal in the parent's place (a picker off a form row, the Links card's add-picker) dropped the parent's pop-back with it; the child inherits it. Returning from the external editor (`^E`) put the caret on line 1 and cleared the undo stack on all four surfaces (Repeater request, Notes, Project description, Intercept editor) — `TextArea#replace_from_outside` keeps the caret and leaves the old text one `^Z` away. `⇥` typed a literal tab into every one-line field that handed printables to `TextField` (a project name, a library entry, an env prefix, the sub-tab rename) — `Key::Tab.to_char` is `\t` — and the field refuses it now so the card can move focus with it. `^Z` reaches the four modal editors (rewriter stub, discover headers, authorize identity, fuzz set values) that only the body editors answered; IME composition under the send-to picker no longer lands in the editor behind it; the Links card comes back on the row the add-picker was opened from; and the find-sub-tab picker opens on the active chip rather than row 0.
- TUI: the list-navigation contract every tab inherits, fixed in the base so no tab has to learn it twice. Re-entering a tab's body from the tab bar, a `Go to …` jump or a `[`/`]` cycle now lands on the pane the tab was last on (`TabController#focus_resume`) instead of its first — reading the Fuzzer RESULTS, `esc`, `↓`, and you were on the URL field; `⇥`/`⇧⇥` keep `focus_first`/`focus_last` as the ring's ends. PgUp/PgDn page by the rows the focused list DREW (`page_rows`), not a screenful of the whole body: Issues, Sitemap, Probe, Rewriter, Diff, Discover and the Intercept queue each skipped four to ten rows a press that had never been on screen — half a page with a preview open — and only History had corrected it; Intercept's page keys now walk the QUEUE, like every other list's, with the held-message preview reachable by wheel and by opening the editor. The wheel scrolls the pane UNDER THE POINTER on every multi-pane tab (`handle_wheel_at`, hit-tested with the same rect each tab's click uses) and never moves keyboard focus to do it — it used to scroll whichever pane held focus, and did nothing at all over the Fuzzer with TARGET focused; a notch or a right-click no longer wipes the toast being read. `⇥ preview` on History, Issues and Probe and `⇥ identity` on Authorize were promises only the mouse kept: the `key.tab?` arms sat in `handle_body_key`, which the Runner never reaches with `⇥` (the focus ring claims it first) — they are `pane_advance` now, and Authorize gains Home/End. A bare letter nothing binds in the current scope says so (`‹x› — nothing bound here · space menu · ? help`) instead of vanishing — typed text that missed its field used to disappear letter by letter except the Global breath keys, which fired (`s` flipped the scope lens, `c` stopped capture) with nothing tying the flip to the typing; modified chords and the named keys stay silent. Also: a filtered History list under capture no longer creeps the highlight down a row per matching flow (the reload moves the viewport with the row, as the unfiltered path did); the Issues preview writes its scroll clamp back so `↑` moves at once after an overshoot; and `j`/`k` walk the READ mode of the Decoder, JWT and Cookie inputs, Notes, the Issues notes and the Project description, where only the arrows did.
- MCP: a query naming a field QL does not implement is refused instead of searched as text. `ql.cr`'s `field_cond` free-texts an unknown field's WHOLE token on purpose, so `list_history{query:"methd:GET"}` ran a literal substring search over method/host/target and came back `[]` with `isError:false` — "this project has no such traffic" and "you spelled `method` wrong" were the same answer, on the one surface whose caller has no screen to notice it on. `strict:true` did not separate them either, and could not: an unknown field COMPILES, so `QL.analyze` files it under `applied`, which is the half `strict` never reports — and that is exactly the capability the exemption in `refuse_unknown_query_fields` rested on ("nor to MCP, whose `strict` argument already offers this"). `list_history`, `list_sitemap`, `probe_scan`, `diff_projects` and `authorize_start` now answer QUERY_SYNTAX naming the field and the spelling meant (``unknown query field `methd:` — did you mean `method:`?``), the way `gori run history` / `sitemap` / `probe` have since #884 and the way the MCP tools that SAVE a query (`create_view`, `create_color_rule`) already did; `lenient:true` is the escape hatch `--lenient` spells there, and `QL.field_shaped?` keeps a pasted URL, an authority (`acme.test:8443`) and a bare word out of it. It also names the one term an agent is most likely to copy off a screenshot: `v:` is the History filter bar's view CHIP, a label and not a term, and `query:"v:all"` used to come back as an empty page. `ql_explain` — the tool whose whole job is to predict this — reported `methd:GET` as an APPLIED term under `refused_by_query_tools:false`; it now carries `unknown_fields` with a `did_you_mean` per name, counts them into that verdict, and says the token is searched as text, which is why it matches nothing. Two loose ends beside it: `probe_scan` now DECLARES the `strict` argument it has always read (passing it was refused as an unknown argument, so the strict path was unreachable there), and `authorize_start` stops answering a misspelled field with "no captured flows matched — widen it", which sends a caller to loosen a query whose only problem is a typo.
- Intercept and extract rules: a condition naming a field a message in flight cannot answer is refused by name, on every surface that submits a complete one. `InterceptFilter::UNSUPPORTED_FIELDS` said "and the whole of that list" while holding `scope` alone, so the eleven other QL fields this backend does not implement — `size`, `reqsize`, `respsize`, `dur`, `stub`, `src`, the four `req.`/`resp.` splits and their aliases — fell through `field_symbol`'s else to FREE TEXT: MCP `intercept_set_filter{query:"dur:>500"}` was accepted, searched method/host/target for the literal string, and held nothing (negated, it held everything), which reads as intercept being broken. That is the exact degradation the file already refuses for `scope:`, and `FIELDS`' own comment already names the fields it is talking about ("`size:`/`respsize:`/`dur:` need an exchange that has FINISHED, and `stub:` needs a capture decision that has not been made"). The list is derived now — every QL field this backend does not implement, aliases resolved first so `res.body:` cannot free-text past the check — so adding a field to the backend retires its refusal in the same edit, and the one sentence the five refusing surfaces share (`intercept_set_filter`, `create_extract_rule`, `gori run intercept filter`, `Bindings#validate`, the TUI's extract-rule form and the intercept bar's note) says WHY per field rather than giving `scope:`'s reason for `dur:`.
- MCP: an OAST call that could not reach the provider is a retryable `NETWORK_ERROR`. `oast_start`, `oast_poll` and `oast_resume` returned their transport failures uncoded, and `Tools#classify` files every uncoded error under `INVALID_ARGUMENT` with `retryable:false` — so a refused connection or a DNS blip told the caller's error policy to fix its arguments and stop, for the one failure class that is transient by construction. `send_request` has always answered a transport failure with `NETWORK_ERROR` + `retryable` (`Tools.send_error_code`); these three agree with it now.
- Docker: the image no longer runs `gori` as PID 1. Linux drops any signal whose disposition is still the default when it is aimed at PID 1, and both shutdown paths were built on exactly that disposition — `App::SignalGuard` restores the terminal and then re-raises the signal at itself so the process dies FROM it and exits the conventional `128+signo`, and `gori mcp` installs no trap at all. In a container the re-raise was discarded, so `docker stop` on the TUI exited `1` instead of `143`, and on `gori mcp` the SIGTERM was dropped outright: the stop burned its whole timeout before SIGKILL (`exit 137`). `tini` is PID 1 now (`ENTRYPOINT ["/sbin/tini", "--", "gori"]`), gori is an ordinary child, and both paths exit `143` at once. `docker run --init` always did the same thing from the outside; baking it in means nobody has to know that. `gori run capture` was never affected — its traps wind down over a channel and never relied on the default disposition.
- Docker: `tzdata` ships in the runner, so `-e TZ=Asia/Seoul` works. Without `/usr/share/zoneinfo` `Time::Location.local` fell back to UTC without erroring, which made the variable a silent no-op and every timestamp UTC; 1.5 MB buys honest local time. The runner also gains `WORKDIR /data`, so a relative path handed to an export or a redirect lands in the mounted volume rather than the container's throwaway write layer, and `README.md`, `snap/` and `scripts/` join `docker/Dockerfile`'s ignore list — nothing under them runs during the build, and whatever is left in the context reaches `COPY . .`, where a single touch costs the full release recompile (a docs-only push to `main` rebuilds in 49s against roughly six minutes for a source change).
- History: the detail drill-in gets a footer strip under the text, and gori's own sentences move out of the pane. `sent by gori — repeater (tui) #4` and the `!` advisories were spliced into the REQUEST pane after the wire bytes, as if they were part of the message — a copy of the pane carried them, and a reader could take the note for a header. They now sit in a strip under the text, below a divider, on every pane (request, response, hex, frames, messages), and the strip leads with the exchange's facts the list had to abbreviate into five-cell columns: `200 · HTTP/1.1 · req 341B · res 152B · 37ms · application/json · 09-05 15:33:00` — status coloured like the list (`ERR`/`ABT`/`···` for a failed, aborted or pending flow, `STUB` in place of the protocol for a short-circuited one), wire sizes rather than the capture-capped blob, and `—` for size and latency until the response lands. The truncation and decode notes stay in the pane's trailer, since they describe the body above them. The strip yields to the text when the terminal is too short to keep three text rows under it, and the click/drag hit-test reads the same rect the render draws, so a click on the strip does not park the caret on the last text row.
- TUI: the Project tab's overview band drops its **Activity** row. It answered a question the tab is not the place to ask — the row read the project db's mtime at the moment `reload` ran, so it re-timed on every entry to the tab and said `34s ago` about nothing more than having just looked; a project's real activity is the ACTIVITY pane's event feed a sub-tab away, and its recency is what the project picker sorts by. `Created` stays, and the narrow tier's folded provenance line is now just `created Nh ago`. `Project#last_modified` is untouched — `gori run project list`, MCP `list_projects` and the picker still report it.
- Repeater and Fuzzer: a WebSocket captured over **HTTP/2** (RFC 8441 extended CONNECT) can now be re-opened and replayed. Seeding a session from one gives the WebSocket tab on all three surfaces instead of a plain HTTP one with a status line explaining what it was not carrying: gori dials h2 (ALPN, or h2c prior knowledge on a plaintext target), waits for the origin's `SETTINGS_ENABLE_CONNECT_PROTOCOL`, re-sends the capture's own extended CONNECT — `:method CONNECT`, `:protocol websocket`, and the `:path`/`:authority`/headers it recorded — and reads the `2xx`, not a `101`, as the socket opening. From there it is the same engine as the HTTP/1.1 path: one framed exchange, one set of capture caps, one transcript, the same masking and frame shapes, so a finding does not depend on which handshake opened the socket. Nothing is fabricated — the `X-Gori-Protocol: websocket` line in the request pane IS the `:protocol` pseudo-header and is folded back into it on send, which also stops a captured extended CONNECT replayed as an ordinary h2 request from carrying gori's own marker line to the origin as a header. Every way it fails is reported rather than showing as a clean run with an empty transcript: an origin that never advertises the setting is a refusal naming it (RFC 8441 §3 forbids sending the stream without it), a non-2xx is a refusal carrying the origin's head, and a `RST_STREAM`, `GOAWAY` or send window that never reopens keeps the frames already exchanged and adds the peer's own stated reason to the result's note. `--http2` on a WebSocket sweep is now refused only against an `Upgrade:` template, since HTTP/2 has no upgrade mechanism and an extended CONNECT is the h2 form; `^V`/`--http` has two stops on such a tab rather than three, because those bytes have no HTTP/1.1 form; and `--ws-keep-key` says it has nothing to keep instead of being ignored (§5.1 carries no `Sec-WebSocket-Key`). Per-message intercept and Match & Replace on messages stay HTTP/1.1-only — both would have to re-frame a DATA payload to a different length (#733).
- Decoder: twenty-seven defects a review of the tab found, across the TUI, the engine, its codecs, `gori run decoder` and the MCP `decode` tool. On the tab: entering it no longer re-runs the chain with hooks on and resets the OUTPUT pane — a persisted `exec:` conversion the project open had deliberately held ran its command the moment the tab was pressed, and a selection left in OUTPUT was thrown away by a round trip through History; `⇧↑`/`⇧↓` select in the INPUT editor (INS and READ) instead of moving, and never leave the pane mid-selection; `^S`/`^O` keep the focused pane rather than landing on INPUT; the CHAIN field scrolls with its caret, so a spec wider than the card is no longer typed blind past the border (a click into the scrolled field lands where it points); `^X` resets the OUTPUT caret and selection with the text it re-encodes, so `y` after a mode flip copies the hex line rather than "" or a fragment at the old offsets; a library save re-derives the ACTIVE conversion with its `exec:` step running, instead of replacing the decode on screen with "chain held", and an OUTPUT whose failure changed to a different failure redraws instead of keeping the old error under a PIPELINE that shows the new one; the PIPELINE previews are bounded and cached (three 16 MiB intermediates cost 1.5 s per frame, on every redraw) and a step's name is clamped inside the card; "copy all" and `y` agree on a failed chain, `^Y` from CHAIN copies the OUTPUT the footer promises, a rename persists at once, `^N`/`^W` drop a sub-tab filter that would hide the session they land on, and saving an empty chain is refused. In the library: a non-UTF-8 spec or name no longer raises out of `Decoder.run` (a Fuzzer marker over a binary region reached it) or out of `Settings.load`, whose blanket rescue then factory-reset every section after `decoder` over one hand-edited chain; an `exec:`-prefixed name is refused by `^S`, the settings parse and the registrar, since the chain grammar runs that token as a command and never reaches the chain it named (a separator inside a name is refused on the same three surfaces now, not only by `^S`); and a `^S`/`^X` whose write the settings file refused puts the library back instead of leaving a name that resolves in this process alone. In the codecs: `powershell-escape` doubles the four Unicode single quotes PowerShell's tokenizer also treats as delimiters; a gzip/zlib stream whose CRC-32/Adler-32 trailer disagrees with its bytes fails as corrupt instead of decoding as a clean Ok; an empty `gunzip` input is refused like its zlib and raw siblings; `base36-decode` folds only ASCII case (a KELVIN SIGN was a digit); and `xml-unescape` leaves `&#x+41;` / `&# 65;` literal. `gori run decoder list` measures its name column (two built-ins are 23 chars) and `--input` beside a 2nd positional is refused rather than silently winning; MCP `decode` takes the whitespace in `input_base64` that `base64-decode` tolerates and never returns more than its byte budget across a split multibyte char. Docs: the `sha256 | hex-encode` example double-encoded a digest, the CLI reference's `decoder` section described sub-tabs it no longer holds, and the guide named `raw-deflate`/`raw-inflate` by their aliases.
- TUI: `^F` search highlighting no longer freezes a pane on a token-dense line. The overdraw asked `String#index` for the next match by CHARACTER offset — which re-walks the line from its first byte on every call — and re-measured each match's column from the row start, so one long line with a match every other column (a minified body is exactly that) cost O(line²) per drawn row, per frame: 16k matches took 250 ms on ASCII and 38 seconds on CJK. The scan is byte-linear now, three forward-only cursors carry the character index, the byte offset and the column, and the loop stops once the band passes the pane's right edge — 0.04 ms and 0.7 ms for the same two inputs (#943).
- Sequencer: five defects an end-to-end audit found, most of them about how many requests a collection makes and how many tokens it admits to having. A run whose LATE samples miss extraction now tops itself up to the goal instead of ending below it — the dispatch loop stopped handing out work as soon as enough samples were merely in flight to *project* the goal, so a miss after that point lowered the projection with nobody left to react (measured 19/20 tokens at concurrency 1 and 35/40 at concurrency 5 against a 50%-extracting origin, on runs with 40 and 80 sends of budget still unspent, the shortfall growing with concurrency); it still lands exactly on the goal with no overshoot, and `max_sends` is still what bounds a descriptor that never matches. `^X` / `sequence_stop` now ends the retry chain too: a retry is a NEW request, and a stop mid-chain kept aiming the rest of them at the target and held the run open for `retries × retry-pause` per worker — 18 extra requests and 490ms in a measurement, up to 1000 requests and ~8 minutes per worker on MCP's ceilings, all after the stop had answered. An EMPTY extraction counts as a miss rather than a collected token: `Set-Cookie: SID=` (what an origin sends to *delete* the session once the replayed one goes stale) hands back `""`, which counted toward the goal and carried no error, while the analysis drops it — so a collection reported "500 collected" over a report reading "0 usable / 500 total · CRITICAL (no usable tokens)". And MCP `sequence_start` stops at twice the goal, like `gori run sequence`, when the caller names no `max_requests`: the server's 100,000-request ceiling was being passed as if it were the caller's own budget, which is exactly how the engine reads that field, so the goal-derived runaway guard never applied and the tool's #1 failure mode — a cookie name that matches nothing — put **100,000 requests** on the wire for a `count: 5` collection (measured; it now sends 10). Separately, the Sequencer tab refuses a `position` range the field does not spell (`100` with the `:B` forgotten, `a:b`, `40:8`) instead of silently reading it as `0:0` and running a real collection in which every sample missed — `gori run sequence --position` and MCP's `position` have always refused the same string (#945).
- Docs: add a capability matrix for protocol and entry-point boundaries, and correct claims about surface parity, WebSocket compression, HTTP/2 downgrades, and agent activity.
- Rewriter: seven defects a review of the tab found. The highlight follows the rule the operator acted on — `c` lands on the copy (it stayed on the original while the copy was appended off screen, so the `e` that usually follows re-opened the wrong rule), and an edit whose scope row re-homed the rule to the other block follows it there instead of staying on the row that slid into its index; a double-click on a rule or extract row opens it in the editor, as every other rule list does; the extract sub-tab's add/delete keys are the chords its strip names (`rewriter.add` / `rewriter.delete`) — the strip followed a rebind while the keys stayed `a`/`d`, and the Colormarker's custom-colours pane had the same gap — and both empty-state lines name the effective chord too; PgUp/PgDn/Home/End move the rule, extract and binding lists; the preview INPUT lets go of the focus when the terminal shrinks the preview pair out of the layout, instead of typing into a pane that is no longer drawn; and the rule form walks past the rows a header op ignores (match, part, and the value row of `remove header`, whose ↵ on the header name now saves) instead of parking the caret on `n/a` rows where ←/→ re-ran the flow scan for nothing (#940).
- Notes: a paste lands as **one edit**, and an idle key no longer dirties the note. Notes was the one multi-line editor still taking a bracketed paste keystroke by keystroke — the Repeater and Fuzzer opted into the bulk path when it landed (`TabController#accepts_bulk_paste?`), and Notes is the tab a captured response, a tool's output or a whole writeup gets pasted *into* — so a 200-line paste paid 200 undo snapshots, 200 highlight rebuilds and 200 frames (quadratic in the paste; the Repeater measured minutes on a 244 KB body), and `^Z` then took it back one character at a time. In INSERT the note now splices the clipboard in as one undo step; READ still refuses a paste rather than running it as commands, exactly as before. Separately, `⌫` at the buffer start, `⌦` at its end and `^Z` on an empty undo stack marked the note dirty while changing nothing, and `dirty?` is not a cosmetic flag there: it is the lock that holds off a peer's reload (`locked?`) and what makes the next esc or sub-tab switch rewrite the whole document — so a stray `^Z` in a clean note silenced every peer refresh until something else saved. The three now dirty only on a real buffer change — and so do the two editors that were still missing the same gate: the Fuzzer template's `⌦`, which re-persisted a template nobody had changed, and the Project description's `⌫`/`^Z`, where the flag is what makes `reload` refuse to re-seed the buffer from the store, so an idle keystroke over clean prose muted every later refresh of that pane including a peer's write.
